Planetary Climate

Day and night, seasons and weather for every body in the system map. Each body has an orbit, a spin, a tilt and a short game year in data/climate.json; the game turns them into a moving line between day and night, seasons that take turns between the hemispheres, and weather cells that drift across the map. Weather is part of the simulation and has mild effects: slower, more fragile rovers, shorter surveys, and storms that keep drop ships on the ground. All numbers are placeholders for balancing.

Section 1

The system at a glance

Sunlight: 1 = Earth at 1 AU, at the mean distance (the range is perihelion to aphelion). Temperatures: the body's mean from its sunlight, albedo and greenhouse; the equator's typical afternoon high and pre-dawn low; the poles of the play map (±°). Day and year are in minutes of play. The system map is a schematic: "map AU" is the body's map distance at the starting scale, "AU" the tuned orbit.

Section 2

Light, dark and seasons by body

One card per body (the duel map is point-symmetric, so each twin shares its card). Seasons run in year order from the northern spring equinox; the south is half a year behind. The bars show how often each kind of weather is picked for a spell in that season; rain falls as snow (and storms as blizzards) where it is below freezing.

Section 3

Weather kinds and their effects

Each kind at full strength; a spell ramps its effects in and out. Rover speed applies to every ground vehicle; survey radius never drops below 1; rover damage scales wear, hazard and flat-tyre chances.

Section 4

How it maps onto the surface

The shared rules that turn a body's numbers into a hex map (climate.json surface).

Section 5

Data checks

Gaps between the climate table and the rest of the design data. The game runs the same and more checks (GameData.Validate).
