Fit Out Your Ship

“The game needs some additional options. You should decide if you are an explorer, a builder, a combatant, etc. Based on this, you can set the crewing/supplies of your ship before you leave home-base. You need to have some of each type of support, but you can go more with one type over another.”

A new step before leaving home. You pick a focus, split the ship’s crew between four divisions and load the hold. Every division and every kind of cargo has a minimum, so no ship leaves without some of each. What you favour moves numbers the game already has by a few percent. An even split plays like today’s game.

After the ship pick, because the ship sets the budget: its crew size, the size of its hold, and whether the cargo is for a colony or for a ship that never lands. Before the system pick, because you asked for it “before you leave home-base”. The system page can then say how the load suits each world, for example how many drop-ship trips the fuel covers on a heavy planet.

The ship is the job

Nothing about the ship pick changes. The ship still decides the mission chain, whether you land, the flight speed and what comes with it (Horizon’s extra colonists and duranium, Cochrane’s spare kits and construction rover).

The focus is how you do the job

Any focus can go on any ship. Each ship suggests one that fits its role and starts on that preset. A survey ship with a Scientist focus is allowed; it is still a survey ship.

The officer’s department

The officer mockup has four departments: Exploration, Defense, Maintenance, Archaeology. The four crew divisions here are the same four things, in the same colours. I propose one pick, made here: your department is your focus’s division.

Fixed for the match

The fit-out is set once, before the first mission. It is part of the game’s set-up, like the ship. The Earth supply ship, when it exists, can top up cargo later; it does not change the crew split.

Rules The whole idea in four steps

1 · Pick a focus

Explorer, Builder, Combatant, Scientist or All-rounder. A focus loads a preset, gives one fixed perk, and its own division must hold at least 30% of the crew.

2 · Split the crew

The ship’s real crew goes into four divisions. Each needs at least 10%. A division’s numbers move 0.4% for every point its share is above or below a quarter: +10% at half the crew, −6% at the minimum.

3 · Load the hold

The hold is counted in containers and its size depends on the ship. Each kind of cargo has a minimum. Past that you choose: more satellites, more metal, more lab gear, more fuel.

4 · Even is today

A quarter in every division changes nothing. Tests, the sandbox and the balance runner keep playing the game as it is. A focus tilts a game; it does not decide it.

The crew’s effect depends on shares, not head counts, so a 40-person survey ship is not weaker than a 350-person flagship. Head count only sets how finely you can split: on the six-person Armstrong each person is a sixth of the ship.

Focus Why each one exists

Explorer

Leads with Exploration · your word

Finding things is half the colony game today: rovers surveying, satellites sweeping, scout drones, and missions that ask for exactly that (Ground Truth, First Flight, Beyond the Ridge). On a ship that never lands it is scanning and charting.

Perk. The landing reveals 5 hexes around the hub instead of 3. In space: long-range scans reach 400 units instead of 320.

Builder

Leads with Engineering · your word

Building, repairing and the parts economy are the other half: build times, repair crews, the Machine Shop, wear. On a ship that never lands the same people are the engine room, so it means shorter flights.

Perk. The Colony Hub’s workshop makes spare kits 30% faster. In space: the engines are tuned before leaving, flights 8% shorter.

Combatant

Leads with Defense · your word

There is no combat yet, so this focus is thin today. It has a few real uses now: wildlife, ruin defences, losing crew when the air fails. It is the focus that grows most when combat is built.

Perk. Vehicles take half damage from wildlife and ruin defences. In space: inspections and escorts need 20 s alongside instead of 30.

Scientist

Leads with Science · added

Research, digs, studying a civilization and first contact are whole systems in the game, and none of your three covers them. Meridian’s entire mission is this. It is also the home for the Archaeology department.

Perk. Research from mission rewards +20%. In space: studying a civilization takes a quarter less time.

All-rounder

No lead division · added

An even split is today’s game exactly, so it needs a name. It is what you get if you skip the page, and what tests and the sandbox use. No perk and no 30% rule: you may still move people around.

Left out for now: a Trader or Diplomat focus (trade only exists after first contact, so it sits inside Scientist), and a Medic focus (too little for it to do; medics are part of Science).

The ranges are the two ends of what the screen allows: a division at its 10% minimum (−6%) and at 70% with the other three at their minimums (+18%). A focus preset puts its division at about 45%, which is +8%.

Space The four ships that never land

Tereshkova, Armstrong, Bastion and Meridian use the same screen and the same four divisions. The crew’s numbers act on the star-system layer instead of the surface. The cargo list is shorter: no building materials, vehicles or colonists, because nothing goes down. Defense cargo has no minimum on these ships until combat gives it a use.

Tereshkova · survey

40 crew · 12 containers · suggests Explorer

Exploration crew shorten the scan recharge and the 15 s sweep of each new body, which is most of The Long Survey. Satellites aboard matter directly: Eyes Everywhere asks for satellites at two bodies, and each one you did not bring costs stock.

Armstrong · courier

6 crew · 6 containers · suggests Builder

Every run is on a clock, so engineers and reaction mass are the obvious load. With six people there is no fine tuning: three engineers is half the ship. The small hold is the point of the courier.

Bastion · patrol

85 crew · 14 containers · suggests Combatant

Defense crew shorten the time alongside a ship in Traffic Check and Escort. Exploration crew help the Picket’s scans. This is the ship that gains most when combat exists; until then its Combatant fit-out is honest but small.

Meridian · envoy

350 crew · 20 containers · suggests Scientist

Science crew shorten the study of the civilization and lower the culture-shock chance. A listening post aboard covers the Listening Post mission; medicine and gifts make first contact safer.

Space chains have no rewards yet, so the Scientist perk is the study-time one on these ships. If the chains later pay out research or standing, the +20% applies there too.

Build What it would take

Data

  • One new file, data/missions/fit_out.json, next to ship_choice.json: the rules, the four divisions, the focuses with their presets and perks, the cargo lines, and per ship the hold size and suggested focus.
  • Each lever is a named entry (drive_speed, build_time, research_rate …) with its division and direction, so tuning is a data edit and shows in the DATA tab.

Sim

  • A FitOut record (focus, four crew counts, cargo counts) passed to MatchSetup.CreateSystemGame(…, shipId, fitOut). Checked against the floors and budgets; a bad one falls back to the ship’s preset. Hashed only when set, like the ship pick.
  • One FitOutProfile per player, built once: a multiplier per lever, read at the places that already multiply (SpeedPerTick, BuildTicksOf, WorkRate, research, sweeps, SystemShip.Speed).
  • Cargo uses the ship pick’s start_bonus and start_units path. No new commands: it is set-up, not an order.

Game screens

  • MainMenu.FitOut.cs: this page, between Choose Your Ship and the system pick. GameSetup.FitOut carries it into the match.
  • System pick: one line per body on how the load suits it (drop-ship trips the fuel covers at that gravity).
  • In game: the focus and the division percentages on the ship’s card, so a tester can see why a number differs.

Tests

  • An even split gives the same hashes as today for every balance order and every ship chain.
  • Floors and budgets refuse bad fit-outs; every lever moves the right way and by the data’s amount; same fit-out twice = same hashes.
  • Each focus preset on each ship still finishes its chain.

Build first: the two colony ships only. The focus, the crew split and five levers that already have a multiplier beside them (drive speed, build time, repair speed, research rate, satellite sweep), plus the cargo that needs no new code (materials, fuel, spare kits, provisions, extra rover). That is one sim session and one UI session, and it is enough to feel whether a focus tilts a game the right amount. Then the four space ships, then the small additions (satellites aboard, field armour, probes). Defense grows when combat is built.
